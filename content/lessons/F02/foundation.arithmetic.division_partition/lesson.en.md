# Division: Equal Sharing and the Inverse of Multiplication

## 1. The Challenge
You have $24$ counters to share equally among $6$ learners. How many does each person receive?
Why can every division statement be checked by a multiplication statement?

## 2. Predict Before Calculating
If $24 \div 6 = 4$, which multiplication statement must be true?
- $4 \times 6 = 24$
- $4 + 6 = 24$
- $24 \times 6 = 4$

## 3. Mathematical Definition: Inverse of Multiplication
For non-negative integers $a, b \in \mathbb{N}_0$ with $b > 0$, the exact quotient:
$$a \div b = q \iff q \times b = a$$
Division answers the question: *what factor must multiply the divisor $b$ to produce the dividend $a$?*

### Why Division by Zero is Undefined
1. **Case $a \neq 0$:** If $a \div 0 = q$, then $q \times 0 = a$. But for all $q$, $q \times 0 = 0 \neq a$. No solution exists.
2. **Case $a = 0$:** If $0 \div 0 = q$, then $q \times 0 = 0$. This holds for *every* number $q$, so no unique quotient exists.
Therefore, division by zero is strictly undefined over all number systems.

## 4. Two Visual Interpretations of Division
1. **Partitive Division (Sharing):** Splitting $a$ objects into $b$ known groups. $q$ is the size of each group.
2. **Quotitive Division (Grouping):** Packaging $a$ objects into groups of size $b$. $q$ is the number of groups formed.

## 5. Worked Example: Checking with Multiplication
To compute $36 \div 4$:
1. Formulate the inverse relation: what number $q$ satisfies $q \times 4 = 36$?
2. From the $4$-times multiplication facts: $9 \times 4 = 36$.
3. Therefore: $36 \div 4 = 9$.

## 6. Non-Example Trap: Order Matters
Unlike addition and multiplication, division is **not** commutative:
$$a \div b \neq b \div a \quad (\text{e.g. } 12 \div 3 = 4 \neq 3 \div 12 = \frac{1}{4})$$
The dividend (amount to be divided) and the divisor (number of parts or group size) play completely distinct mathematical roles.

## 7. Reflection
Division reverses multiplication in the exact same way that subtraction reverses addition: each operation undoes the effect of its foundational counterpart.

### Prerequisite check
Multiply single-digit numbers and recognize that $6$ groups of $4$ make $24$.

### Representation
Place $15$ tokens on a table. Form equal piles of $3$ until all tokens are used. You form $5$ piles: $15 \div 3 = 5$. Checking: $5$ piles $\times 3$ tokens $= 15$ tokens.

### Complete a partly worked problem
Complete using multiplication: $42 \div 6 = \underline{\hspace{1cm}}$ because $\underline{\hspace{1cm}} \times 6 = 42$.
Self-check: $42 \div 6 = 7$ because $7 \times 6 = 42$.

### Independent practice
1. Calculate $48 \div 8$ and write the multiplication equation that proves your answer.
2. Explain why $0 \div 7$ is $0$, but $7 \div 0$ is undefined.

### Explanation
Explain to a classmate how to divide $56$ by $8$ if they only know their $8$-times multiplication table.

### Transfer
A baker has $72$ cookies. She packs them into boxes of $6$ cookies each. How many full boxes can she pack?
Solution: $72 \div 6 = 12$ boxes. Check: $12 \times 6 = 72$.
