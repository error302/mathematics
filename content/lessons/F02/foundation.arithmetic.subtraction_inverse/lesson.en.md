# Subtraction: Difference & Additive Inverse

## 1. The Challenge
A train journey from Town $A$ to Town $B$ is $12$ kilometers long. The train has already traveled $7$ kilometers.
How many kilometers remain before reaching Town $B$?

## 2. Predict Before Calculating
Notice how this question can be framed in two opposite ways:
1. "What remains after taking $7$ away from $12$?" ($12 - 7 = ?$)
2. "What must be added to $7$ to reach $12$?" ($7 + ? = 12$)

Why do both questions ask for the exact same number?

## 3. Mathematical Definition: Inverse of Addition
For any two numbers $a, b$:
The **difference** $a - b$ is defined uniquely as the number $c$ such that:
$$a - b = c \iff c + b = a$$
Subtraction is not an independent primitive operation; it is the **inverse operation** of addition.

## 4. Two Mental Models for Subtraction
1. **Take-Away (Partition):**
   You have a set of size $a$, remove a subset of size $b$, and count what remains.
2. **Comparison (Distance):**
   On a number line, $a - b$ is the directed distance (displacement) from point $b$ to point $a$.
   *(This model easily generalizes later to negative integers $\mathbb{Z}$ and real numbers $\mathbb{R}$).*

## 5. Non-Commutativity
Unlike addition, subtraction is **not commutative**:
$$a - b \neq b - a \quad (\text{unless } a = b)$$
For example:
$$12 - 7 = 5 \quad \text{while} \quad 7 - 12 = -5$$
Order strictly matters in subtraction.

## 6. Worked Example: Finding the Missing Addend
To compute $43 - 28$:
Think: "What do I add to $28$ to reach $43$?"
- Step 1: Add $2$ to reach the nearest ten: $28 + 2 = 30$.
- Step 2: Add $13$ to reach $43$: $30 + 13 = 43$.
- Step 3: Total added: $2 + 13 = 15$.
Therefore: $43 - 28 = 15$.

## 7. Reflection
By recognizing subtraction as the search for a missing addend ($b + ? = a$), mental calculations become dramatically faster and less prone to borrowing errors.
