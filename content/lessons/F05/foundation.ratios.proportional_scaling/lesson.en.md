## 1. Multiplicative Comparison vs Additive Comparison

Consider two runners: Runner A completes $4$ laps while Runner B completes $6$ laps.
There are two distinct ways to compare these quantities:
1. **Additive comparison:** Runner B ran $2$ more laps than Runner A ($6 - 4 = 2$).
2. **Multiplicative comparison (Ratio):** Runner B ran $1.5$ times as many laps as Runner A ($6 / 4 = 3 / 2 = 1.5$).

When we scale a recipe, resize a diagram, or calculate travel speed, the additive difference changes, but the multiplicative ratio remains invariant.
A **ratio** $a : b$ expresses this fixed relative scale between quantities.

---

## 2. Proportions and the Scale Factor

A **proportion** is an equation stating that two ratios are equal:
$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$$

This holds if and only if there exists a nonzero rational scale factor $k$ such that:
$$c = k \cdot a \quad \text{and} \quad d = k \cdot b$$

Multiplying both sides of $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$ by the common denominator $b \cdot d$ yields the fundamental **cross-product property**:
$$a \cdot d = b \cdot c$$

---

## 3. Two Systematic Solution Methods

### Method 1: The Within-Ratio Unit Rate
Find the value per single unit:
$$\text{Unit rate} = \frac{b}{a}$$
Then multiply by the new quantity: $d = c \times \text{unit rate}$.

### Method 2: The Between-Ratio Scale Factor
Determine the multiplier between corresponding terms:
$$k = \frac{c}{a} \implies d = k \cdot b$$

### Worked Example:
A recipe requires $3$ eggs for every $8$ cups of flour. How many cups of flour are needed for $12$ eggs?
$$\frac{3 \text{ eggs}}{8 \text{ cups}} = \frac{12 \text{ eggs}}{x \text{ cups}}$$
Scale factor from $3$ to $12$ is $12 \div 3 = 4$.
Applying to the denominator:
$$x = 8 \times 4 = 32 \text{ cups of flour}$$

---

## 4. Misconception Alert: The Additive Trap

When presented with $3 : 8 = 12 : x$, a very common misconception is:
"From $3$ to $12$ we added $9$, so we add $9$ to $8$ to get $17$."
Let's check if $\frac{3}{8} = \frac{12}{17}$:
$$3 \times 17 = 51 \neq 8 \times 12 = 96$$
The cross-products fail! Ratios demand constant multiplication (scaling), not constant addition.
