# Direct Addition on the Soroban

## 1. The Challenge
You have already set the number $2$ on the units rod (two lower beads pushed up to the beam).
Now you want to add $1$.
Can you simply push another lower bead up, or do you need to touch the upper bead or another rod?

## 2. Predict Before Practicing
Predict:
- What is $2 + 1$?
- How many lower beads remain unused at the bottom of the rod after adding $1$?

## 3. What is Direct Addition?
**Direct Addition** occurs when the rod already has enough inactive beads available to satisfy the addend directly, without needing:
- The upper $5$-bead complement ("friend" numbers to $5$).
- Carrying over to the tens rod ("big friend" numbers to $10$).

### The Direct Lower Bead Condition
If a rod currently has $L$ lower beads active ($0 \le L \le 3$) and you want to add $k$ lower beads ($1 \le k \le 4 - L$):
$$L + k \le 4$$
You can add $k$ directly by pushing $k$ lower beads up with your thumb.

## 4. Worked Example: 1 + 2 = 3
1. **Clear the abacus** (neutral state $0$).
2. **Set 1:** Push $1$ lower bead up with your thumb.
3. **Add 2:** There are $3$ inactive lower beads at the bottom. Push $2$ more lower beads up with your thumb.
4. **Read:** $3$ lower beads touch the reckoning beam. Result: **3**.

## 5. Direct Addition Involving the Upper Bead: 2 + 5 = 7
1. **Set 2:** Push $2$ lower beads up with your thumb.
2. **Add 5:** The upper bead is currently UP (inactive, value $0$). Pull the upper bead DOWN to the beam with your index finger.
3. **Read:** One upper bead ($5$) plus two lower beads ($2$) equals $7$. Result: **7**.

## 6. Where Direct Addition Stops
Try computing $3 + 2$.
There is only $1$ inactive lower bead remaining at the bottom! We cannot push $2$ lower beads up.
*(Solving $3 + 2$ requires the $5$-Complement Rule: $+2 = +5 - 3$, which we will master in Course A03!)*

## 7. Reflection
Mastering direct addition trains your fingers and eyes to immediately recognize when beads can be moved directly without mental decomposition.
