# Integer Exponents and Powers

## 1. The Multiplicative Pattern

Multiplication is repeated addition. Exponentiation is repeated multiplication:

$$b^n = \underbrace{b \times b \times \dots \times b}_{n \text{ times}}$$

Where:
- $b$ is the **base** (the value being repeatedly multiplied).
- $n$ is the **exponent** or **power** (the count of factors).

### Decreasing Exponents and Division

Observe what happens when we decrease the exponent step by step:
- $2^4 = 16$
- $2^3 = 8$ (divided by 2)
- $2^2 = 4$ (divided by 2)
- $2^1 = 2$ (divided by 2)

Continuing this division pattern logically:
- $2^0 = 2 \div 2 = 1$
- $2^{-1} = 1 \div 2 = \frac{1}{2}$
- $2^{-2} = \frac{1}{2} \div 2 = \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{2^2}$
- $2^{-3} = \frac{1}{4} \div 2 = \frac{1}{8} = \frac{1}{2^3}$

## 2. Core Exponent Laws

For any non-zero base $b \neq 0$ and integers $p, q$:

1. **Zero Exponent Rule**:
   $$b^0 = 1 \quad (b \neq 0)$$
2. **Negative Exponent Rule**:
   $$b^{-n} = \frac{1}{b^n} \quad (b \neq 0)$$
3. **Product Rule**:
   $$b^p \cdot b^q = b^{p+q}$$
4. **Quotient Rule**:
   $$\frac{b^p}{b^q} = b^{p-q} \quad (b \neq 0)$$

## 3. Avoiding the Sign Error

A negative exponent denotes **inversion of operation** (multiplication becomes division), not a negative number.
For example:
$$5^{-2} = \frac{1}{5^2} = \frac{1}{25} > 0$$
Contrast this with a negative base:
$$(-5)^2 = 25 \quad \text{and} \quad -5^2 = -(5^2) = -25$$
Always pay attention to whether the minus sign belongs to the base, the exponent, or the entire expression.
