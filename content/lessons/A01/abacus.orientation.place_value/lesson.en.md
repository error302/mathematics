# Orientation to the Soroban Abacus & Place Value

## 1. The Soroban Instrument
The modern Japanese **soroban** is a decimal calculating instrument structured around rods with a $1:4$ bead arrangement:
- A horizontal **reckoning bar** (beam) separates the beads into two decks.
- **Upper deck (Heavenly bead):** Each rod has **one upper bead** worth **5** units when pushed down toward the beam.
- **Lower deck (Earthly beads):** Each rod has **four lower beads** worth **1** unit each when pushed up toward the beam.

> **Fundamental Rule:** Beads only count toward the value when they touch the reckoning bar!

## 2. Reading Digits 0 to 9 on a Single Rod
- **0:** Upper bead is up (away from bar); all lower beads are down (away from bar).
- **1 to 4:** Push $1, 2, 3,$ or $4$ lower beads up to the bar.
- **5:** Push the upper bead down to the bar; all lower beads down.
- **6 to 9:** Upper bead down ($5$) plus $1, 2, 3,$ or $4$ lower beads up ($5 + 1 = 6$, $5 + 2 = 7$, etc.).

There are exactly $10$ valid states on any rod ($0$ through $9$).

## 3. Place Values Across Multiple Rods
Rods represent place values in base $10$, ordered from highest place on the left to lowest place on the right:
- On a 2-rod abacus: the left rod represents **Tens ($10^1$)** and the right rod represents **Ones ($10^0$)**.
- Example for the number **15**:
  - Tens rod: $1$ lower bead up ($10$).
  - Ones rod: Upper bead down ($5$), lower beads down ($0$).
  - Total: $1 \times 10 + 5 \times 1 = 15$.
