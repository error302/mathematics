# Reading a Mathematical Problem: Words, Quantities, and Unknowns

Mathematics is often called the language of the universe, but mathematical problems usually start in natural language—English, Swahili, Japanese, or Arabic. 

Before we can compute, we must **read**:
1. What quantities are given as fixed facts?
2. What relationships connect these quantities?
3. What is the specific unknown quantity we seek?

## Avoiding the "Keyword Trap"

A common beginner misconception is hunting for isolated keywords:
- Seeing "more" and immediately adding.
- Seeing "share" and immediately dividing.
- Seeing "less" and immediately subtracting.

Consider:
> "Maya has \$20 less than Liam. Maya has \$45. How much does Liam have?"

If you mechanically subtract because you see "less", you calculate $45 - 20 = 25$, which is wrong! Liam has *more* money than Maya: Liam has $45 + 20 = \$65$.

Instead of isolated keywords, identify the **structural equation**:
$$\text{Liam's money} - \$20 = \text{Maya's money}$$
Let $x$ be Liam's money:
$$x - 20 = 45 \implies x = 65$$

## The Four-Step Mathematical Reading Method

1. **State the unknown**: Write down explicitly what $x$ represents in units (e.g. litres, books, dollars).
2. **Tabulate the knowns**: List given quantities with their exact values and units.
3. **Formulate the relation**: Express how the parts combine to form the whole.
4. **Solve and verify**: Compute the exact value and check that it makes sense in the original sentence.
